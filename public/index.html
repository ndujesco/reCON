    Connected data sources

    Both feeds are pulled on a schedule from systems Wema already runs. Nothing is uploaded, nothing is exported to a spreadsheet, and there is no separate portal to sign into: the data is already inside the bank, it has simply never been joined up.

    Data shape

    Reconciliation works on structured records: a reference, an amount, an account, a value date. Records that arrive as free text are never quietly guessed at. They are flagged, matched on amount, account and value date where that is possible, and held for a human to confirm.

    Record explorer

    A transfer, the fee on it, the VAT on that fee and the levy are four separate postings against one instruction. Classifying them before matching is what stops a fee break from reading as a missing transfer.

    Settlement match

    Wema's own postings, matched line by line against the NIBSS NIP settlement report for the session. A reference matches exactly. A record with no reference is matched on amount, account and value date, and is proposed rather than booked.

    Validation findings

    Duplicates, one-sided records, amounts the two sides disagree on, and general ledger balances that did not carry forward. Ranked worst first, each one naming the records it is drawn from.

      General ledger continuity

      Yesterday's closing balance has to equal today's opening balance. Where it does not, money moved without a posting to explain it, and the difference is the amount to go looking for.

      Suspense account monitor

      Funds that arrived but were never swept to the beneficiary account. ReCON ages every entry automatically, so stuck money surfaces on its own instead of being found weeks later during manual reconciliation.
